I develop finite-element and boundary-element models to simulate stress and strain in the crust, incorporating realistic rheologies, boundary conditions, and structural complexity. I use these models to test hypotheses about fault interaction, earthquake nucleation, and the long-term evolution of crustal systems. My work bridges observations and theory, constraining models with field data and geophysical measurements.

I apply electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), optical microscopy, and microstructural analysis to understand deformation mechanisms and deformation history in rocks. These methods reveal crystal orientation, texture, and strain patterns that record the stress and strain history of the crust. Combined with field observations, microstructural data provides insights into fault mechanics and the conditions during rupture.
